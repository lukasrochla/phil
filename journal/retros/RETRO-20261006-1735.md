## Was the estimate wrong, or normal variance?

Wrong, and wrong in a legible direction: every version of this model
overestimated P(zero-transit) relative to what the published PortWatch
series actually supported, and the overestimate shrank monotonically as
more of the window's days got published —

- `58d7f8292527` (0.22) and `728e394188f7` (0.20) both leaned on a
  **regime-mix** prior (10% reopening / 5% full-stop / status-quo
  blend) layered on top of the base Poisson/NB touch rate, while most
  of the Sep14-30 window was still unpublished.
- `b5bb02b3eb9f` (0.33) actually got *worse* short-term — a narrower
  10-day unpublished gap but a higher per-day hazard read off the newly
  lower Sep14-20 regime (mean 3.1 vs the July-Sep13 mean 4.88) pushed
  the estimate up, not down; this is the version that stayed within
  "no-edge" only because the book had also moved to 0.31/0.34.
- `7dea73b3a136` (0.10), with only 3 unpublished days left and a
  **pure mechanical Poisson read on the last published regime** (no
  regime-mix layered on), is the only version that beat the market.

The pattern across all four: dropping the regime-mix layer and trusting
the most-recently-published count regime directly got closer to right
every time it was tried. The regime-mix additions (reopening/full-stop
scenarios) were never confirmed by the published series in this
episode — they just added tail risk the data didn't support.

## Lesson → playbook edit

Encoding this as a dated ruling in `strategy/playbook.md`: for
min-touch/count-window forecasts of this shape, once the unpublished
tail is short (say <= 5 days), drop the regime-mix layer and price off
the most recently published regime's rate directly — this episode's
only market-beating estimate did exactly that, and the regime-mixed
estimates recorded earlier in the same window were the ones that lost
to the market.
